JPG, PNG or WebP: choose for the image you actually have

The best format depends on what is in your image and where the file will be used. A small photo, a transparent logo and a screenshot need different decisions.

1. Photographs and gradients: test JPG against WebP

Both formats can discard detail to reduce storage. Start with the original, make a delivery copy, and inspect subtle gradients, hair, foliage and high-contrast edges. A quality value of 80 is an encoder setting; it does not mean that exactly 80% of the original quality remains. The same value in JPG and WebP is not an equal-quality comparison.

Our test chart contains gradients, lines and text so you can see some of these differences. It is a synthetic illustration, not a photograph. Results for a phone photo may be quite different.

2. Text and screenshots: avoid choosing by size alone

PNG stores the encoded pixels without lossy compression. This makes it a useful starting point for interface screenshots and diagrams. When converting them to JPG or lossy WebP, inspect small text and narrow colored lines. A preview scaled to fit a page can hide artifacts; download the result and view it at 100% before sharing it.

3. Transparent images: what a JPG changes

JPG has no alpha channel. FastImgConvert fills transparent areas with white when creating JPG files. White edges that were invisible on a white page can become obvious on a dark page. Use PNG or a compatible WebP output when you need to keep transparency.

See the transparent badge example: both the original PNG and the white-background JPG are available to download. The checkerboard in the preview is a display background, not part of the transparent PNG.

4. Format conversion has limits

  • Converting a JPG to PNG does not restore detail previously removed by JPEG compression.
  • Resizing a small image upward adds pixels through interpolation; it does not recover the original scene.
  • This canvas-based tool makes a still image from animated GIF, WebP or AVIF inputs. It is not an animation converter.
  • Source decoding depends on the browser. HEIC, RAW and SVG are not supported inputs in this version.
  • Original EXIF, camera information and editing metadata are not preserved by this tool. Keep the source for archival or print work.

A repeatable comparison

Keep one untouched source. Export JPG and WebP at your chosen quality, check both at full size, and choose a file that meets your destination's requirements. Do not repeatedly convert the last compressed result.
